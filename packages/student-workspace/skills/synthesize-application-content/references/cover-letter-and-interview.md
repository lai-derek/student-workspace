# Cover letters, outreach, and interviews

For a cover letter, connect a few strong experiences to actual role needs instead of repeating the resume. Verify current organization details before using them.

For a mentorship request or other personal outreach, give the recipient a specific reason for the conversation. Connect something from their work, interests, or stated approach to a supported interest, experience, or plan of the user's, then make the actual question clear. Personal common ground can be as useful as professional overlap. Avoid reciting their biography, flattering their success, or implying a shared experience the user has not had. The recipient-specific message should add to the general profile rather than reproduce it.

Make uncertainty concrete when it is the reason for asking. Choosing which experiences would help someone discover a direction is a different request from preparing for a role they already chose. Do not replace that distinction with interchangeable language about guidance, growth, or navigating opportunities. Keep the greeting and closing ordinary; the substance should carry the personality.

For an interview answer, make the situation, personal action, result, and relevant reflection understandable. Use a short speaking outline when useful. Do not invent dialogue, motivations, or a perfect resolution.
